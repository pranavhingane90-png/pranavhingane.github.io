Interactive Signals & Systems Practical Lab

Perform real-time continuous and discrete signal experiments, convolution calculations, spectral FFT analysis, sampling reconstruction, and noise filtering directly in your browser.

Practical 1: Signal Synthesis & Representation

Generate, visualize, and alter Continuous $x(t)$ and Discrete-time $x[n]$ fundamental signals.

Mathematical Theory Reference:

Continuous sinusoidal standard representation: $x(t) = A \cdot \sin(2\pi f t + \phi)$.
Discrete-time sequence representation: $x[n] = A \cdot \sin(\Omega n + \phi)$, sampled at discrete integer indices $n$.

Practical 2: Linear Convolution Visualizer

Compute and observe the step-by-step convolution response $y[n] = x[n] * h[n]$ between input signal $x[n]$ and impulse response $h[n]$.

Mathematical Theory Reference:

Discrete Linear Convolution Equation: $y[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[k] \cdot h[n - k]$.
Length of output sequence $y[n]$ is given by $L_y = L_x + L_h - 1$.

Practical 3: Fourier Spectrum Analysis (FFT)

Decompose composite time-domain signals into their corresponding discrete frequency spectrum.

Mathematical Theory Reference:

Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT): $X(k) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x(n) \cdot e^{-j 2\pi k n / N}$.

Practical 4: Nyquist-Shannon Sampling & Aliasing

Analyze the effect of sample rate variation relative to Nyquist criteria ($f_s \ge 2 f_{max}$).

Mathematical Theory Reference:

Nyquist Criterion: To prevent aliasing, sampling frequency $f_s$ must satisfy $f_s \ge 2 \cdot f_{max}$. If $f_s < 2 f_{max}$, high frequencies alias into lower frequency bands.

Practical 5: Signal Noise Suppression & FIR Filtering

Pass corrupted noisy signals through digital Moving Average Low-Pass FIR filters.

Mathematical Theory Reference:

$M$-Point Moving Average Filter Equation: $y[n] = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} x[n-k]$. Smooths out rapid high-frequency noise transients.
